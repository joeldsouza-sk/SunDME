Denials & follow-up

Turn denial follow-up into a connected worklist

Bring the reason, supporting record and next action together so teams can follow the whole story.

Sample blog · Prepared for the SunDME website.

A denial worklist is more useful when the person working it can see the claim’s context. A reason code alone rarely tells the full story. The order, supporting documents, payment history and earlier actions help explain what needs attention.

Capture more than the status

Record the denial reason and source, the claim or service line involved, the current owner and the next action. Keep supporting correspondence with the relevant record so a new team member can understand what has already been tried.

Use a consistent follow-up record

  • What reason did the payer provide?
  • Which claim, service line and order are involved?
  • What supporting information has been reviewed?
  • What action is needed, who owns it and when is it due?
  • What response or resolution has been received?

Trace issues back to the source

When a recurring issue points to an intake or documentation gap, share the finding with the upstream team. A short, specific review is more actionable than a broad request to “improve clean claims.”

Separate completed actions from resolved claims

A phone call or corrected submission is an action, not necessarily a resolution. Record the next checkpoint and retain the outcome so the team can distinguish work performed from an issue actually closed.

Keep the whole story in view

SunDME’s structured denial capture connects follow-up context to the claim and supporting artifacts. Review the workflow with your team to define ownership, status conventions and handoff expectations.

This sample article does not prescribe appeal deadlines or guarantee payment. Confirm the applicable payer process for each claim.
