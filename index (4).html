Fictional case study

One order. A clearer path across the team.

An illustrative look at how a DME provider could organize intake, delivery and claim preparation around one connected record.

Fictional case study. The provider, scenario and observations below are invented to demonstrate the page format. They do not describe a SunDME customer or verified results.

The scenario

Harbor Home Equipment is a fictional DME provider with separate intake, fulfillment and billing teams. In this example, a respiratory equipment order arrives with patient details, a referral and supporting documents spread across several locations.

The challenge

The intake team knows what is missing, but the fulfillment team cannot easily see that context. Later, billing staff have to retrace the order to find the supporting record. Staff spend time asking colleagues for updates that could sit with the order itself.

A proposed connected workflow

  1. Intake: organize patient details, payer information and the referral in the connected record.
  2. Documentation: link supporting files to the patient and order, and record outstanding review items.
  3. Order review: make the current status, owner and next action visible to the responsible team.
  4. Delivery: connect item assignments, delivery details and applicable delivery evidence to the order.
  5. Claim preparation: review claim data with a traceable path back to the source documents and fulfilled order.
  6. Follow-up: capture a denial or payment question against the relevant claim and supporting context.

What the team would review

CheckpointReview question
Intake readinessCan staff identify missing information and who owns the next action?
Handoff clarityCan the receiving team understand the order without repeating the initial review?
TraceabilityCan billing staff find the relevant order, delivery and supporting documents?
Exception follow-upDoes each unresolved issue have an owner, next action and follow-up date?

The intended outcome

This scenario illustrates a goal: a shared, traceable workflow that gives each team the context it needs. No implementation, measured improvement or financial result is claimed. A real case study would require customer approval and validated evidence.
